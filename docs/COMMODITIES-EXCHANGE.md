# Commodities exchange and supplier contracts (Prompt 5)

The Penny Exchange is a real order book over real inventory and the real ledger. There is no fabricated activity: every price, volume and shortage on screen is read from executed trades, the live book, production plans and funded consumer budgets.

## What exists
* **Order book** (`server/engine/market.ts`): limit orders reserve coins (buy) or units (sell) first; price-time priority with deterministic ordering (price, then order id); partial fills across several counterparties; execution at the resting order's price; cancellation releases exactly the unfilled reservation; expiry (`expires_tick`) releases it automatically at the start of the tick; idempotent placement.
* **Order kinds**: `limit` (rests, optional 1–60 day expiry), `market` (protected: price cap/floor = best opposite price ± slippage, immediate-or-cancel, refused if the book is empty), `direct` (buy only from one chosen supplier, immediate-or-cancel). A market order never becomes a disguised limit order.
* **Safety**: no trade with the same company; funds/stock reservations cannot be spent twice (enforced in code and by database CHECK constraints); limit prices outside 0.1×–10× of the reference price are refused; every command is validated server-side, idempotent and refuses non-owned companies; a deliberate sell order withdraws the company's automatic daily listing so the stock can back it.
* **Fees**: the seller pays the exchange fee (default 1%, configurable). It is a **transfer** to the Penny Exchange account (`acct:state:penny-exchange`). Each tick the exchange burns `exchangeBurnBps` of what it holds (explicit `sink_fee` transaction "Penny Exchange fee burn") and sweeps the rest to the state treasury as a transfer. Nothing is minted to settle trades.
* **Market data** (`market_stats`, one row per city × commodity × day): trades, volume, value, fees, open/high/low/close/VWAP, best bid/ask and listed units at close, estimated demand, consumer units and contract units. The terminal's directory, depth, history, supplier comparison and shortage/surplus badges are all derived from it and from the live book. Demand is labelled an *estimate* (funded consumer budgets + production plans).
* **Retail goods** (pizza, café drinks, timber for public works) are sold over the counter, not on the exchange: their "supply" is shop shelf stock and their gap is real unmet consumer demand; the UI says so and points to company pricing.
* **Commodities**: wheat, milk, flour, cheese, timber, pizza, café drinks, energy (existing registry) plus **coffee beans** and **packaging** (new; used by cafés and pizza restaurants, supplied by the state provisioner at a premium in bounded quantities — no producer industry exists yet). Recipes and the commodity registry remain the single source of truth.
* **NPC and state participants** (unchanged rules, now exposed): NPC firms post asks from their stock at full-cost-plus prices, buy inputs through the book (cheapest first, ≤150% of reference), have finite inventory and explicit cash budgets, adjust price and output to stock and sales; the state provisioner holds bounded stock (`stateSupplyPerTick`/`stateSupplyCap`) priced at 140% of reference.
* **Supplier contracts** (`server/engine/contracts.ts`): the player proposes as buyer or supplier; the NPC/state counterparty answers by published rules (price covers its cost and asking price, volume ≤ 50% of its daily output/need; reason always shown). Deliveries settle atomically on schedule (goods + coins + fee) before the day's listings, or are recorded as missed (supplier stock, buyer funds or storage). Three consecutive misses end the contract; there is no monetary penalty. Cancellation takes effect after 3 days' notice; deliveries due earlier are honoured. Contract trades appear in company statements and market statistics (separately labelled).
* **Company procurement**: the company's supply panel shows each ingredient's stock, market and state-fallback price, with a *Find supplier / Compare* action that opens a supplier comparison scoped to that company, then a direct purchase or limit order through the normal quote → confirm flow.
* **Opportunity finder**: `GET /api/opportunities` now also returns `shortages` (demand vs what producers plan per day, listed units, recent price, ingredient cost, producers) computed live; shown as a "Bristol commodity markets" table.
* **Local markets**: orders, trades and statistics carry `city_id` (Bristol only for now). There is no cross-city transfer; future intercity trade must add transport and cost.
* **Developer dashboard** (`/dev/economy` → "Penny Exchange"): fees held/collected/burned/swept, volumes and prices per commodity, open orders and reservation consistency check, who trades with whom, NPC activity, contract health, failed settlements and anomaly detection (reservation mismatch, self-trade, crossed book, stale expiry, price outlier, shortage, supplier concentration).

## API
`GET /api/exchange/commodities`, `/api/exchange/commodities/:id` (depth, history, recent trades, suppliers, own orders/trades/holdings, contract options), `/api/exchange/orders`, `/api/exchange/trades`, `/api/exchange/contracts`; `POST /api/quote` (limit, market, direct), commands `commodity.order` (+ `orderKind`, `slippageBps`, `expiresInTicks`, `fromCompanyId`), `order.cancel`, `contract.propose`, `contract.respond`, `contract.cancel`.

## Schema (migration `0003_exchange`, additive)
`orders`: `kind`, `expires_tick`, `end_reason`, `city_id`, `only_company_id`; `trades`: `city_id`, `fee_tx_id`; new `market_stats`, `supply_contracts`, `contract_deliveries`. Seed patch `exchange-v1` creates the exchange account and the coffee/packaging commodities and recipe inputs.

## Known limitations
No authentication or multi-player isolation yet (one demo player), so "player-to-player" is exercised between the player's own companies, NPCs and the state; the pending-offer path (`contract.respond`) is built but only reachable once a second player exists. NPCs do not post resting bids, so best-bid is usually empty; NPC counterparty contracts are accepted by rule, not negotiated. Coffee and packaging have no producer industry. Thin margins mean undercutting wholesalers can go bankrupt (see the simulation report).
